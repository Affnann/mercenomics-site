What it is

The United States government hires private companies to guard embassies, military bases, immigration detention centres, courthouses and federal buildings. Each of these contracts is reported to USAspending.gov, the government's public spending database, under the product and service code S206.

This dataset holds every contract action coded S206 from October 2007 to September 2026: 215,353 actions on 50,726 contracts, $79.3 billion in obligations, of which $15.8 billion was for work outside the United States. It shows where the money went by year, by country where the work was done, by agency and by company.

What it cannot be used to say

  • The amounts are obligations, not payments: money the government commits to pay. Later changes can take money back, so 18 percent of rows are negative, and they are kept as reported.
  • Amounts are in current dollars, not adjusted for inflation.
  • The 2026 financial year is probably incomplete for the Department of Defense, whose contract records arrive with a 90-day delay.
  • "Guard" is broad. The code covers armed and unarmed guards, at home and abroad, including detention officers. Other security work coded elsewhere, such as surveillance, is not included.
  • Some recipients appear only as undisclosed or miscellaneous awardees, because USAspending reports them that way.
  • Companies are grouped by the parent company reported for each action, so a firm that was later bought may appear under its old or its new owner.

How it was checked

  • For every financial year, the sum of the cleaned rows matches USAspending's own published total for S206 contracts to the cent.
  • Every row has code S206 and a financial year from 2008 to 2026, and no contract action appears twice.
