Security companies on official lists

Data  >  Security companies on official lists

A directory of private security companies built only from official lists: ICoCA member companies, UK Security Industry Authority Approved Contractors, UK companies registered for private security activities, SEC filers under the guard and armoured-car industry code, and recipients of US federal guard contracts, with each list's identifier, status and dates and links between entries with the same name and country.

What it is

There is no single official register of private security companies. Each authority keeps its own list for its own purpose: the International Code of Conduct Association lists the companies that have signed up to its human rights code, the UK Security Industry Authority lists the contractors approved under its voluntary scheme, Companies House lists every UK company by the activity it declares, the SEC files listed companies by industry, and the US government records who receives its guard contracts. This dataset puts the five lists side by side, keeps each list's own identifier, status and dates, and marks the companies that appear on more than one.

There are 20,636 entries. Most are small UK companies: 16,923 live UK companies give private security as an activity, and two thirds of them were incorporated in 2020 or later. ICoCA's 198 member companies are mostly headquartered in conflict-affected countries, led by Iraq (42), the United Kingdom (16) and Somalia (11). The lists rarely overlap outside the UK: 493 of the 719 SIA Approved Contractors share a name with a UK company registered for private security, but only 10 ICoCA members also hold US guard contracts under the same name, among them Triple Canopy, SOC and Michael Stapleton Associates.

A link is a name match, not proof. Entries are linked when their names match after legal-form words are removed and the country agrees; this does not prove they are the same legal entity. Every link is listed so it can be checked. No personal details are included: UK locations are given only as post town and postcode district.

What it cannot be used to say

  • It cannot say how many private security companies a country has: the lists cover different things, and most countries have no list here.
  • Being on a list is not a mark of quality. ICoCA membership and SIA approval are voluntary schemes with their own standards; Companies House and SEC codes are chosen by the companies themselves, and a company coded for private security may never have traded.
  • It cannot show that a company missing from a list is unlicensed or unregulated.
  • A name match is not proof of identity. Different companies can share a name, and the same company can appear under different names (a parent and its subsidiary are not linked).
  • Not every US guard-contract recipient is a security company: facilities-management firms and prison operators also hold guard contracts.

How it was checked

  • Every record of every list is in the directory once, and no identifier appears twice within a list.
  • For all 718 SIA entry pages read, the approval end date matches the register list (one page returned a server error).
  • Every SEC company's own filing record gives the guard and armoured-car code, and every Companies House company kept has the private security activity code.
  • The US guard-contract recipients add up to the us-guard-spending total, less its two placeholder entries, to $0.1 million.
  • No telephone numbers appear, UK locations are only town and postcode district, and every link joins entries with the same name and country.
