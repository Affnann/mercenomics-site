Japan traffic-guard pay rates

What it is

Every year Japan's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism sets the public works design labour rates: the daily wage that government cost estimates assume for each construction trade in each prefecture. Two of those trades are traffic guards working for private security companies. Guard A has passed the national grade 1 or grade 2 traffic-guarding exam; Guard B is any other security company guard directing traffic.

This dataset turns the ministry's yearly PDF tables into one file for all 47 prefectures, and adds the Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare's monthly figures on job openings and job seekers in security occupations.

What it cannot be used to say

  • The rates are wages, not prices. The ministry states that they cover only the wage paid to the worker and leave out the security company's overheads, insurance, training and profit, so they are well below what a client pays per guard-day.
  • They are rates used to estimate public contract costs, not a record of what each guard was actually paid.
  • Special rates set after the 2011 earthquake (Iwate, Miyagi and Fukushima) and under a COVID-19 measure in the 2021 financial year are marked in the file.
  • Almost every prefecture rises more than 15 percent between 2012 and 2013. This matches the ministry's own figure for that year and is not a data error.
  • The job-openings figures cover security occupations in general, not traffic guards alone, and only jobs registered at Japan's public employment offices (Hello Work). They are national, not by prefecture.

How it was checked

  • Every PDF was read twice with two different programs, and all 1,410 values agree.
  • The job-openings series was checked where two occupation classifications overlap, from April 2022 to March 2023; for security occupations they give identical figures.
